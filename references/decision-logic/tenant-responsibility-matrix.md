# Tenant Responsibility Matrix

How to determine whether a maintenance issue is owner-responsible, tenant-responsible, or shared. This matrix is referenced during triage and before dispatch to set expectations about who pays.

## The Default Rule

If in doubt, treat it as owner-responsible and dispatch. Let the PM sort out chargebacks after the fact. Never delay a repair because responsibility is unclear. Health, safety, and habitability always come first.

## How This Matrix Works

Each company configures their own responsibility rules during onboarding (see `references/onboarding/setup-interview.md`, Section 9). The categories below are common defaults. If the company has uploaded a custom responsibility matrix or lease language, defer to that. If nothing was configured, use these defaults.

## Categories

### Always Owner-Responsible

These are owner-responsible regardless of lease terms, tenant behavior, or cause:

- All building systems: plumbing supply lines, drain lines (main), sewer, electrical wiring, breaker panels, HVAC systems, water heaters
- Structural elements: walls, ceilings, floors, roof, foundation, exterior doors, windows (frame and glass)
- Owner-provided appliances: mechanical failure, normal wear and tear
- Smoke detectors and CO detectors: replacement and maintenance (some jurisdictions allow tenant battery replacement, but the device itself is always owner)
- Habitability items: heat, hot water, weatherproofing, working locks on exterior doors, pest control for infestations (not single sightings in some leases)
- Mold remediation on building surfaces
- Water damage from building system failures (pipe burst, roof leak, water heater failure)

### Commonly Tenant-Responsible (Check Lease)

These are often assigned to the tenant in leases, but enforcement varies. Always check the company's configured policy:

- **Drain clogs at a single fixture** (bathroom hair, kitchen grease). Main line clogs are always owner.
- **Lockouts from lost keys**. Many companies charge the tenant for locksmith calls when the tenant lost their key. Broken locks are always owner.
- **Light bulbs** (tenant-accessible fixtures). Specialty or hard-to-reach fixtures are owner.
- **HVAC filter replacement** (some leases require tenants to replace monthly). A clogged filter causing a system failure is still owner-coordinated for the repair.
- **Pest control for minor issues** (a single ant, a fly). Infestations are owner.
- **Smoke detector batteries** (tenant replaces batteries, owner replaces the unit).
- **Damage caused by tenant or tenant's guests**. The repair is still owner-coordinated, but the tenant may be charged.

### Grey Areas

These come up often and don't have a universal answer. The coordinator should flag for PM review rather than making the call:

- **Toilet clogs**: most PMs treat as standard maintenance. Some leases assign to tenant.
- **Garbage disposal misuse**: tenant put something wrong in it. Repair is owner-coordinated, but some leases allow chargeback.
- **Tenant-owned appliances**: not a maintenance issue unless it's causing damage to the property (e.g., a tenant washer leaking onto the floor).
- **Window screens**: some leases assign to tenant.
- **Minor cosmetic damage**: scuff marks, small nail holes, paint touch-up. Usually addressed at move-out, not during tenancy.

## How the Coordinator Should Handle Responsibility

1. **During triage**: always provide troubleshooting guidance regardless of responsibility. A tenant-responsible drain clog still gets the same diagnostic tree. Don't withhold help.
2. **Before dispatch**: if the issue is tenant-responsible per the company's policy, inform the tenant before dispatching. Use the suggested communication: "I want to let you know upfront that per your lease, [issue type] calls are the tenant's responsibility costwise. I'm still happy to send someone out. Just wanted you to know before we dispatch."
3. **Never argue responsibility with a tenant.** State it once, note it in the work order, and move on. If the tenant pushes back, escalate to the PM. The coordinator's job is to fix the problem, not litigate the lease.
4. **Never delay a dispatch because of a responsibility dispute.** Dispatch, note the responsibility classification in the work order, and let the PM handle billing.

## Overrides

Even if something is tenant-responsible by default, it becomes owner-responsible when:

- The issue creates a habitability concern (a tenant-responsible drain clog that causes sewage backup)
- The issue risks property damage beyond the tenant's unit (a clog in an upper floor unit affecting units below)
- The issue is a safety concern regardless of cause
- The tenant has reported it multiple times and it recurs (suggests a building issue, not tenant behavior)

## Configuration

During onboarding, the agent collects the company's responsibility rules. These override the defaults above. If the company uploaded lease language or a custom matrix, parse it and apply it. If a specific issue type isn't covered by the company's configuration, fall back to the defaults in this document.
