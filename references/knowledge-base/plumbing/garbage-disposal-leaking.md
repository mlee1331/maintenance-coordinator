# Garbage Disposal Leaking

## Symptoms covered

- Water dripping from the bottom of the garbage disposal unit
- Water leaking from the top flange (where disposal connects to sink drain)
- Water leaking from the side discharge (where dishwasher or drain pipe connects)
- Puddle under the sink when disposal runs or when water drains through it
- Water stains or mold on the cabinet floor beneath the disposal

Does NOT cover: disposal not working (see KB-PLUMB-001), leak from sink drain pipes downstream of the disposal (see KB-PLUMB-008), leak from the faucet or supply lines (see KB-PLUMB-006).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. Where does the water appear? Under the sink on the cabinet floor, or dripping from a specific spot on the disposal?
2. Does it leak all the time, only when the disposal runs, or only when water drains through? [PHOTO under the sink]
3. Is it a slow drip or a steady stream?
4. How old is the disposal? (If unknown, that's fine.)
5. Is there any visible corrosion or rust on the unit?

**Classification check**: A disposal leak is not urgent unless the water volume is significant enough to cause cabinet or flooring damage. If it's a steady flow (not just a drip), or if the cabinet floor is warped/moldy from prolonged leaking, bump to urgent.

## Diagnostic decision tree

```
START
│
├─ WHERE is the leak coming from?
│   ├─ BOTTOM of the disposal unit
│   │   └─ Internal seal failure. The unit's internal seals have failed
│   │       and the housing is leaking. Not repairable. The disposal
│   │       needs replacement.
│   │       └─ DISPATCH plumber or handyman for disposal replacement.
│   │
│   ├─ TOP FLANGE (where the disposal meets the sink drain opening)
│   │   ├─ Most common disposal leak location.
│   │   ├─ The mounting ring or plumber's putty has failed.
│   │   ├─ At Level 3+: is the mounting ring visibly loose?
│   │   │   └─ Tenant should NOT try to re-mount a disposal (heavy,
│   │   │       awkward, can damage sink).
│   │   └─ DISPATCH. Tech will re-seat the flange with new putty or
│   │       replace the mounting hardware.
│   │
│   ├─ SIDE DISCHARGE (where the dishwasher hose or drain pipe connects)
│   │   ├─ At Level 3+: is the hose clamp or slip-nut connection
│   │   │   visibly loose or dripping?
│   │   │   └─ If a hose clamp (dishwasher connection): tighten the
│   │   │       clamp screw with a flat screwdriver. Run the dishwasher
│   │   │       and check.
│   │   │       └─ Stops leaking → RESOLVED
│   │   │       └─ Still leaking → DISPATCH
│   │   │   └─ If a slip-nut connection (drain pipe): hand-tighten
│   │   │       the nut. Don't over-tighten.
│   │   │       └─ Stops leaking → RESOLVED
│   │   │       └─ Still leaking → DISPATCH
│   │   └─ If tenant can't identify or reach → DISPATCH
│   │
│   └─ UNCERTAIN where it's coming from
│       └─ Ask for [PHOTO]. If still unclear, DISPATCH for inspection.
│           Plumber will identify the source and repair.
│
├─ Is the disposal OLD or visibly CORRODED?
│   └─ Disposals last 8-12 years. If it's old and leaking from the
│       bottom, replacement is the only fix and is more economical
│       than repeated repairs.
│
└─ All disposal leaks → DISPATCH unless side connection tightening
    resolves it.
```

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

1. **Tighten dishwasher hose clamp** (Level 3+): If the leak is from the dishwasher connection on the side of the disposal, tighten the hose clamp screw with a flat screwdriver. Run the dishwasher to test.

2. **Tighten drain pipe slip-nut** (Level 3+): If the leak is from the drain pipe connection on the side, hand-tighten the slip nut. Don't use pliers (can crack the nut).

3. **Place a container**: Put a bowl or small bucket under the drip to prevent cabinet damage until dispatch arrives.

All other disposal leaks are not tenant-fixable.

## When to dispatch

- Bottom leak (internal seal failure, replacement needed)
- Top flange leak (re-mounting needed)
- Side connection leak that doesn't stop with tightening
- Any leak causing cabinet damage or mold
- Tenant unable or unwilling to check connections

## Vendor category to dispatch

Plumber or handyman (disposal work is straightforward). If the unit needs replacement, a plumber or appliance-capable handyman handles removal and installation. Replacement disposals are inexpensive.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- Place a bowl or pan under the drip
- If the leak is steady (not just a drip), avoid running water through that side of the sink until repaired
- Don't run the disposal if it's leaking from the bottom (water will flow faster under power)
- Check for cabinet floor damage. If the wood is soft or moldy, note for the vendor (may need cabinet repair too)

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible. Disposal leaks are wear-and-tear on owner-provided equipment. Even if the tenant has been rough with the disposal, the leak itself is a maintenance issue.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Location of the leak (bottom, top flange, side connection)
- Volume (drip vs. steady flow)
- Whether it leaks constantly or only when running/draining
- Age of the disposal if known
- Whether there's visible corrosion
- Photo showing the leak location
- Whether cabinet floor is damaged
- Tenant availability

## Suggested tenant communication

**Standard**: "Disposal leaks are pretty common, especially on older units. I'll get someone out to take a look. In the meantime, put a bowl under it to catch the drip, and try not to run water through that side if you can avoid it."

**If replacement needed**: "If the disposal is leaking from the body itself, it usually means the internal seals are done. The tech will likely recommend replacing the whole unit. That's a quick job, usually under an hour."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Bottom leaks on disposals are always replacement, never repair. The internal seals are not serviceable.
- Top flange leaks are the most common and usually the cheapest fix (re-seat with putty, tighten mounting ring).
- If the disposal is over 10 years old and needs any significant repair, recommend replacement instead. A new standard disposal is cheap to replace and not worth repairing when it will fail again soon.
- Check whether the disposal is owner-provided. If tenant-owned (they bought and installed it), it's their responsibility to repair/replace. Only dispatch if the leak is damaging the owner's property.
- Prolonged disposal leaks cause cabinet floor rot and mold under the sink. If the tenant reports the leak has been going on for a while, flag for the vendor to assess cabinet damage.
