# Toilet Leaking at Base

## Symptoms covered

- Water pooling around the base of the toilet on the floor
- Water seeping from under the toilet when flushed
- Toilet rocks or wobbles and water appears at the base
- Stain or damage to ceiling below a second-floor bathroom (indicating a base leak from above)

Does NOT cover: toilet overflowing from the bowl (see KB-PLUMB-002), condensation on the outside of the tank dripping to the floor (see condensation note below), water leaking from the supply line connection behind the toilet (supply line issue, dispatch directly).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. When does the water appear? Only when you flush, or all the time?
2. How much water? A small puddle, or spreading across the floor? [PHOTO]
3. Does the toilet rock or wobble when you sit on it?
4. Is there a second floor above with a bathroom, or is this the ground floor?
5. Is there a bathroom below this one? (If so, check for ceiling water stains below.)

**Classification check**: A toilet leaking at the base is almost always a wax ring failure. It means potentially contaminated water (sewer water) is on the floor. Classify as urgent. In multifamily with a unit below, this is more urgent because it can cause ceiling damage and health concerns downstairs.

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

This issue is not tenant-fixable. Wax ring replacement requires removing the toilet from the floor. The only tenant actions are damage mitigation:

1. **Contain the water**: Place old towels around the base. Replace as needed.
2. **Minimize flushing**: If another bathroom is available, use that one instead.
3. **Do NOT tighten the floor bolts**: The decorative caps at the base of the toilet cover the bolts that hold it to the floor. Over-tightening these can crack the porcelain. Don't touch them.

Exception: if the source is actually condensation on the tank (common in humid climates), it's not a leak. Advise the tenant to run the bathroom exhaust fan during and after showers.

## When to dispatch

- Water at the base during or after flushing (wax ring, always dispatch)
- Toilet wobbles (shifted off ring)
- Water is discolored or has odor (sewer water on floor)
- Unit below reporting ceiling water from this bathroom
- Supply line dripping that won't tighten

## Vendor category to dispatch

Handyman for standard wax ring replacement. Plumber if the flange is damaged (common in older units) or if additional plumbing work is needed. Wax ring swaps are straightforward handyman work. If the subfloor is water-damaged from long-term leaking, additional repair (flooring, subfloor) may be needed. Flag for NTE if there's any sign of long-term damage.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- Use a different bathroom if available
- Place towels at the base, replace when wet
- Minimize flushes
- If unit is on a second floor and water is reaching the unit below, consider shutting off the supply valve behind the toilet and having the tenant use another bathroom until repair. This stops the leaking entirely.

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible. Wax ring degradation is normal wear. Even if the toilet wobbles due to a tenant being rough, the repair is still owner responsibility (it's a fixed building component).

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- When water appears (at flush, continuously, or both)
- Volume of water
- Whether toilet rocks/wobbles
- Photo of the base area
- Whether there is a unit below and any reported damage there
- Whether another bathroom is available in the unit
- Tenant availability
- Whether supply valve was shut off as mitigation

## Suggested tenant communication

**Initial response**: "Water at the base of a toilet usually means the wax seal underneath needs replacing. That's a plumber job. I'll get one scheduled. In the meantime, try to use your other bathroom if you have one, and put some old towels around the base to catch any water when you do flush."

**If only one bathroom**: "Since that's your only bathroom, I'll prioritize getting the plumber out quickly. Use it normally but put towels down. The water might look concerning but it's not going to get worse suddenly."

**If water reaching unit below**: "I need to get this handled quickly because it's affecting the unit below you too. I'm going to have you turn off the water valve behind the toilet for now (turn it clockwise until it stops). You can turn it back on briefly when you need to flush, then turn it off again. I'll get the plumber out today."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Wax rings fail eventually on every toilet. Average lifespan is 15-30 years depending on use and conditions. This is normal maintenance, never tenant-caused.
- If the toilet wobbles, the previous wax ring replacement may have been done poorly (not shimmed properly). Note this for the vendor.
- In multifamily: a second-floor toilet base leak is urgent because it causes ceiling damage below. Check whether the downstairs unit has reported water. If so, coordinate access for both units.
- After-hours: a base leak is not a same-night emergency unless water is actively flooding. The tenant can shut off the supply valve and use another bathroom overnight. Schedule for first thing next morning.
- Subfloor damage: if the floor around the toilet feels soft or spongy, there may be water damage to the subfloor beneath the vinyl/tile. The plumber will assess, but this can increase scope significantly. Flag for NTE and possible secondary dispatch (flooring).
