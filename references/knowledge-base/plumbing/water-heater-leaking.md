# Water Heater Leaking

## Symptoms covered

- Water pooling around the base of the water heater
- Water dripping from the top connections of the water heater
- Water coming from the pressure relief valve (T&P valve) on the side
- Water heater in a closet or pan and the pan is full of water
- Water damage on floor near the water heater

Does NOT cover: no hot water without a leak (see KB-PLUMB-004), water heater making noises but not leaking (sediment buildup, schedule flush), gas smell near water heater (Emergency severity, dispatch immediately).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. Where is the water coming from? The bottom of the tank, the top connections, or a valve on the side? [PHOTO]
2. How much water? A small puddle, steady drip, or actively flowing?
3. Is the water hot or cold?
4. Is the water heater in a utility closet, garage, or open area? Is it on an upper floor?
5. Is there a drain pan under the water heater? Is it full?

**Classification check**: A leaking water heater is always urgent minimum.
- If the leak is from the tank body (bottom) → the tank has corroded through. It will only get worse. Urgent dispatch.
- If the leak is significant volume → risk of flooding. Urgent dispatch.
- If the water heater is on an upper floor → flood risk to the unit below. Urgent dispatch.
- If from the T&P valve → could indicate dangerous pressure buildup. Dispatch. Do NOT ask tenant to cap or plug the valve.

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

Not repairable by tenant. The only tenant actions are mitigation:

1. **Turn off the water supply valve**: Find the cold water inlet valve on top of or near the water heater. Turn clockwise to stop feeding water to the tank. This significantly slows a tank leak.

2. **Turn off the heat source**: Gas heater: turn the gas valve to OFF or PILOT. Electric heater: flip the breaker to OFF. An empty or low tank that's still heating is dangerous.

3. **Contain the water**: If there's no floor drain, place towels or buckets. If the water heater has a drain pan, check if the pan's drain line is flowing to an appropriate location.

## When to dispatch

- Always. Water heater leaks are always dispatched.
- Tank body leak (bottom): urgent (replacement needed, will only get worse)
- Top connection leak: urgent (pressurized water, continuous)
- T&P valve discharge: semi-urgent (safety concern, pressure issue)
- Upper-floor water heater: urgent (flood risk to units below)

## Vendor category to dispatch

Plumber. Water heater work (repair or replacement). If the unit needs replacement, the plumber handles removal and installation. Note that replacement may require permits in some jurisdictions and may trigger code upgrades (expansion tank, new venting, earthquake strapping in CA/seismic zones).

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- Shut off the water supply valve to the heater (stops feeding water)
- Shut off the heat source (gas to OFF/PILOT, or breaker OFF)
- Place towels/buckets if no floor drain
- Note: tenant will have no hot water after shutting off the heater. If dispatch is next-day, this is acceptable (one night without hot water is manageable).

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible. Water heater maintenance and replacement are always owner responsibility.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Location of leak (bottom, top, T&P valve)
- Volume (drip, puddle, flowing)
- Type of water heater (gas/electric, tank/tankless)
- Age if visible (date on label)
- Whether tenant shut off water supply and heat source
- Whether the heater is on an upper floor (flood risk below)
- Photo of the leak and overall unit condition
- Whether a drain pan is present and functioning
- Tenant availability

## Suggested tenant communication

**Active leak, providing instructions**: "A leaking water heater needs attention quickly. Here's what I need you to do right now: find the water supply valve on or near the top of the heater and turn it clockwise to shut it off. Then, if it's gas, turn the gas knob to OFF. If it's electric, flip the water heater breaker off. That'll slow the leak way down while I get a plumber out."

**After tenant shuts it off**: "Perfect, that should slow it down. You won't have hot water until the plumber gets there, but I'm getting someone out as quickly as I can. Towels or a bucket under the drip in the meantime."

**Likely replacement**: "Given where it's leaking, the plumber may recommend replacing the whole unit rather than trying to patch it. I'll flag that for the property owner's approval. Water heater replacements usually take a few hours once approved."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- A tank body leak means the tank has corroded through. There is no repair. Only replacement. Alert the PM that this is a replacement situation and flag for NTE.
- If the water heater is old (12+ years for tank, 20+ for tankless), proactive replacement may be recommended by the plumber. Owner decision.
- T&P valve discharge is a SAFETY DEVICE. Never advise anyone to cap it, plug it, or restrict it in any way. If it's releasing water, that means pressure or temperature is too high. The plumber needs to diagnose why.
- Upper-floor water heaters are higher risk. A full tank (40-50 gallons) leaking through a floor can cause significant damage to the unit below. Prioritize these.
- After replacement: the plumber may need to address code requirements (expansion tank, seismic strapping in CA, proper venting for gas, new supply lines). This can add to the job cost. Include in NTE estimate.
- Some jurisdictions require permits for water heater replacement. The plumber should handle this, but confirm.
