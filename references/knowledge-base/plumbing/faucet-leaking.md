# Faucet Leaking or Dripping

## Symptoms covered

- Faucet drips when turned off
- Faucet leaks from the base/handle area when turned on
- Faucet handle is loose and water seeps out
- Faucet sprayer leaking

Does NOT cover: faucet won't turn on at all (supply issue), water leaking under the sink from supply lines or P-trap (see KB-PLUMB-007), water pressure problems throughout the unit.

## Intake questions for tenant

1. Which faucet? (Kitchen, bathroom sink, bathtub/shower.)
2. Where is the water coming from? Dripping from the spout when off, or leaking from the base/handle when on? [PHOTO]
3. How fast is the drip? Occasional drip, steady drip, or a thin stream?
4. Is the water getting on the floor or into the cabinet below?
5. How long has this been going on?

**Classification check**: A faucet drip is standard priority in almost all cases. Reclassify as urgent only if: water is actively flowing to the floor and the tenant cannot shut off the supply valves under the sink, or the drip is so fast it's a thin stream and is wasting significant water (owner pays water bill).

## Diagnostic decision tree

```
START
│
├─ Is water reaching the FLOOR or CABINET?
│   ├─ Yes → can the tenant place a towel or container to catch it?
│   │   └─ Yes → contained for now. Standard dispatch.
│   │   └─ No (volume too high) → can the tenant turn off supply valves
│   │       under the sink? (Turn clockwise to close.)
│   │       └─ Valves closed, water stopped → standard dispatch
│   │       └─ Valves won't close or don't exist → URGENT dispatch
│   └─ No → standard dispatch. Continue triage if aggressiveness allows.
│
├─ Is it dripping from the SPOUT when turned off?
│   ├─ This is almost always a worn cartridge, washer, or valve seat.
│   │   Not tenant-fixable. DISPATCH.
│   ├─ Confirm: does it drip from both hot and cold, or just one?
│   │   (Helps the plumber know which side to rebuild.)
│   └─ Note in work order: single-handle or two-handle faucet, and
│       make/model if visible (often on the base or behind it).
│
├─ Is it leaking from the BASE or HANDLE when on?
│   ├─ This is typically an O-ring or cartridge issue.
│   │   Not tenant-fixable. DISPATCH.
│   ├─ [PHOTO] of where the water appears when the faucet is running.
│
├─ Is the HANDLE loose and water seeps when turned?
│   ├─ At Level 4+: ask if the tenant can tighten the handle.
│   │   Some handles have a set screw (small Allen screw under a cap)
│   │   or tighten by twisting the base.
│   │   └─ If tightened and leak stops → RESOLVED
│   │   └─ If still leaking or handle won't tighten → DISPATCH
│   └─ Below Level 4 → DISPATCH
│
└─ All other faucet leak types → DISPATCH
```

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

Most faucet leaks are not tenant-fixable. They require cartridge or washer replacement, which involves shutting off water, disassembling the faucet, and replacing internal parts. This is plumber work.

1. **Tighten a loose handle** (Level 4+ only): Look for a small cap on the handle (often marked H or C). Pop it off. Tighten the screw underneath with a screwdriver or Allen wrench. If the handle is stable and the leak stops, done.

2. **Turn off supply valves to stop active drip**: If the drip is fast enough to be wasteful or causing damage, turn the supply valves under the sink clockwise to shut off water to that faucet until the plumber arrives.

## When to dispatch

- All spout drips (worn internal parts)
- All base/handle leaks
- Loose handle that doesn't resolve with tightening
- Any leak causing water to reach the floor or cabinet

## Vendor category to dispatch

Handyman for standard faucet repairs (cartridge swap, washer replacement, tightening) and straightforward faucet replacement. Plumber for supply line work or if the issue involves valve body damage. Faucet work is well within a capable handyman's scope and cheaper than a plumber.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- For drips: place a cup or small container in the sink to reduce the sound. Not a damage risk if contained in the basin.
- For leaks reaching the floor or cabinet: place towels, check twice daily, and turn off supply valves if the leak is significant.
- If tenant can shut off supply valves and the faucet is not needed for basic function (e.g., bathroom sink when kitchen works fine), shutting off until repair is a reasonable short-term solution.

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible. Faucet wear is normal mechanical degradation of owner-provided fixtures. Even if the tenant was rough with the handle, the internal parts are the owner's responsibility to maintain.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Which faucet (location)
- Single-handle or two-handle
- Where the leak is (spout, base, handle)
- Make/model if visible
- Photo of the leak
- Whether supply valves are accessible and functional
- Whether water is causing damage (floor, cabinet)
- Tenant availability

## Suggested tenant communication

**Standard drip, scheduling repair**: "Got it. A dripping faucet isn't an emergency but it shouldn't be ignored either. I'll get a plumber scheduled within the next few days. In the meantime it's fine to use the faucet normally."

**If causing damage**: "Since the water is getting into the cabinet, let's contain that. Can you turn the supply valves under the sink clockwise to shut them off for now? I'll get a plumber out as quickly as I can."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Faucet drips are high-volume, low-urgency calls. They almost never warrant same-day dispatch unless water is causing damage.
- If the owner pays the water bill, a running faucet costs money. A steady drip can waste 5+ gallons per day. Note this in the work order if relevant.
- Bathtub/shower faucet leaks are the same fundamental issue but harder to repair (wall access sometimes required). Flag to the plumber if it's a tub/shower.
- If the faucet is old (visibly corroded, outdated style) and the property owner has a budget for upgrades, the plumber may recommend replacement over repair. Flag for NTE.
- For after-hours: never dispatch a plumber after hours for a faucet drip unless it's an uncontrollable stream. Schedule for next business day.
