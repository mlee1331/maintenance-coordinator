# Toilet Not Working

## Symptoms covered

- Toilet will not flush at all
- Toilet flushes weakly or incompletely
- Toilet runs constantly (water keeps flowing after flush)
- Toilet handle loose or broken
- Toilet clogged and plunger not resolving
- Toilet overflowing

Does NOT cover: toilet leaking at the base (that's a wax ring issue, dispatch directly), sewage smell without visible clog (could be a vent issue, dispatch), toilet cracked or physically damaged (dispatch).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. What's happening with the toilet? Won't flush, runs constantly, clogged, or overflowing?
2. Is there water on the floor right now?
3. Is this the only toilet in the unit?
4. If clogged: have you tried a plunger?

**Classification check**: If this is the only toilet in the unit, classify per severity-classification.md. A single-toilet unit with no working toilet is a High severity (habitability) issue. If the toilet is actively overflowing onto the floor and the tenant cannot stop it, treat as Medium at minimum (water damage risk).

## Diagnostic decision tree

```
START
│
├─ Toilet is OVERFLOWING
│   ├─ IMMEDIATE: tell tenant to remove the tank lid and push down the
│   │   flapper (rubber piece at the bottom of the tank) to stop water flow.
│   │   OR: turn the water supply valve (behind/below the toilet) clockwise
│   │   to shut off water.
│   ├─ Once water is stopped: is the bowl clogged?
│   │   └─ Yes → plunger steps below
│   │   └─ No (water just wouldn't stop running) → running toilet steps below
│   └─ If tenant cannot stop the water → this is now urgent. DISPATCH.
│
├─ Toilet is CLOGGED (won't drain)
│   ├─ IMPORTANT: if the bowl is full or nearly full, tell the tenant
│   │   do NOT flush again. Flushing a full bowl causes overflow. Plunge first.
│   ├─ Does the tenant have a plunger? (Flange/cup style works best.)
│   ├─ Plunger technique: place plunger fully over the drain opening at the
│   │   bottom of the bowl, push down and pull up firmly 10-15 times keeping
│   │   the seal. Pull plunger out. Does water drain?
│   │   └─ Yes → flush to test. RESOLVED.
│   │   └─ No → try one more round of plunging, 15-20 pumps.
│   │       └─ Still no → DISPATCH
│   └─ If tenant has no plunger → DISPATCH
│       (Note: at Level 4-5, suggest hot water + dish soap method:
│       pour a few squirts of dish soap into the bowl, then slowly
│       pour a pot of hot but NOT boiling water from about 6 inches
│       above the water line. Do NOT pour from height into a full
│       bowl -- it will splash. Wait 10 minutes. Try flushing.
│       Works on soft clogs.)
│
├─ Toilet RUNS CONSTANTLY
│   ├─ Lift the tank lid. Look inside.
│   ├─ Is the flapper (rubber piece at bottom) seated properly?
│   │   └─ If it's lifted or crooked → push it down into place.
│   │       └─ If running stops → RESOLVED
│   │       └─ If it won't stay → flapper is warped/deteriorated. DISPATCH.
│   │           (At Level 5: flapper replacement is a common guided repair.
│   │           cheap part, 10-minute install. Consider mailing one.)
│   ├─ Is the fill valve (tall piece on the left) spraying water?
│   │   └─ Spray into the overflow tube is normal. Spray everywhere is not.
│   │       → DISPATCH
│   ├─ Is the water level above the overflow tube?
│   │   └─ The float is set too high. Look for an adjustment screw or clip
│   │       on the float arm/column. Adjust down so water stops 1" below
│   │       the overflow tube top.
│   │       └─ If adjusted and running stops → RESOLVED
│   │       └─ If tenant can't find adjustment → DISPATCH
│   └─ If none of the above → DISPATCH
│
├─ Toilet WON'T FLUSH (handle does nothing)
│   ├─ Lift the tank lid. Is the chain connected from the handle arm to
│   │   the flapper?
│   │   └─ If chain is disconnected → reconnect it. Hook it back on the
│   │       handle arm. Test flush.
│   │       └─ If it flushes → RESOLVED
│   │   └─ If chain is broken → DISPATCH (or at Level 5, a new chain/
│   │       handle kit is a cheap guided repair)
│   ├─ Is the handle arm broken or spinning freely?
│   │   └─ Yes → DISPATCH
│   └─ If tenant cannot remove tank lid or isn't comfortable → DISPATCH
│
└─ Toilet FLUSHES WEAKLY
    ├─ Is the water level in the tank low (below the fill line)?
    │   └─ Adjust the float up slightly to increase water level.
    │       → Test flush. If improved → RESOLVED.
    ├─ Are the rim holes clogged? (Water should flow from under the rim.)
    │   └─ Hard water buildup can clog rim holes. Suggest vinegar soak:
    │       pour vinegar under the rim, let sit 30 minutes, scrub with an
    │       old toothbrush, flush.
    │       → If improved → RESOLVED
    │       → If still weak → DISPATCH (may be a vent or drain line issue)
    └─ If none of the above → DISPATCH
```

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

1. **Stop an overflow**: Turn the supply valve clockwise (behind the toilet, near the floor) to shut off water. OR lift the tank lid and push the flapper down.

2. **Plunge a clog**: Use a flange plunger. Seal over the drain, push and pull firmly 10-15 times. Check if water drains. Flush to confirm.

3. **Fix a running toilet (flapper)**: Lift the tank lid. Push the flapper down into its seat. If it won't stay, it needs replacement (dispatch or guided repair at high aggressiveness levels).

4. **Reconnect the flush chain**: Lift the tank lid. Hook the chain back onto the handle arm. Test.

5. **Adjust water level**: Find the float adjustment (screw or clip on the fill valve). Adjust up for more water per flush, down to stop overflow into the tube.

## When to dispatch

- Plunger does not clear the clog after two attempts
- Toilet is overflowing and tenant cannot stop the water
- Flapper is deteriorated and won't seat (unless Level 5 guided repair)
- Handle mechanism is broken
- Fill valve is spraying erratically
- Toilet is only one in unit and cannot be resolved quickly (habitability)
- Toilet leaking at base (wax ring, not covered in this entry)
- Recurring clogs (may indicate a drain line issue deeper in the system)

## Vendor category to dispatch

Handyman for internal repairs (flapper, fill valve, handle, chain, float adjustment) and simple clogs. Plumber for recurring clogs requiring a snake or camera inspection, wax ring issues, or supply line problems. Most toilet calls are handyman-level work. If the plumber recommends a camera inspection for recurring issues, flag for NTE.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- If clogged and only toilet: locate the nearest public restroom or neighbor who can help. Escalate dispatch timing.
- If overflowing: supply valve OFF. Towels on the floor. If water reached adjacent rooms, prop open doors and run fans to dry.
- If running constantly: turn the supply valve off when not in use. Turn it back on to flush, then off again. Inconvenient but prevents a massive water bill.
- Don't use chemical drain cleaners in toilets. They don't work on toilet clogs and can damage the porcelain and wax ring.

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible for toilet mechanical failure (flapper, fill valve, handle, wax ring). Toilet clogs are a grey area in some leases, but most PMs treat them as standard maintenance. Recurring clogs in the same unit may indicate a drain line issue (always owner responsibility) rather than tenant behavior.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Symptom (won't flush, clogged, running, overflowing, handle broken, weak flush)
- Whether this is the only toilet in the unit (habitability)
- Whether plunger was tried and result
- Whether supply valve was shut off
- Whether water got on the floor
- Photo if there's water damage or visible damage to the toilet
- Tenant availability

## Suggested tenant communication

**Clogged, only toilet**: "I know this is frustrating, especially with just one bathroom. I'm getting a plumber out to you as quickly as I can. If you were able to shut off the supply valve, that'll prevent any overflow in the meantime."

**Running toilet**: "A running toilet wastes a lot of water but it's not going to cause damage. I'll get it scheduled. If you want to stop the running sound in the meantime, you can turn the supply valve behind the toilet off. Just turn it back on when you need to flush."

**After overflow with water on floor**: "Since there was water on the floor, keep an eye on the area around the toilet for the next couple days. If you notice any soft spots in the flooring or smell anything musty, let me know."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Always check if this is the only toilet. Single-toilet units with no working toilet trigger habitability classification.
- Toilet overflow with water on the floor is a property damage risk. Treat with urgency even if the tenant manages to stop the water.
- Running toilets waste significant water and can spike the water bill. If owner pays water, urgency is higher from a cost perspective.
- At Level 5, flapper replacement is one of the most successful guided repairs. It's a cheap part available at any hardware store, takes 10 minutes, no tools required. Consider mailing one to the tenant if the timeline allows.
- Never ask a tenant to work on the wax ring, remove the toilet, or do anything that involves lifting the toilet off the floor.
- If the plumber recommends toilet replacement, flag for NTE and owner decision.
