# Water Leak Under Sink (Supply Line or P-Trap)

## Symptoms covered

- Water pooling in the cabinet under the sink
- Water dripping from pipes under the sink when water is running
- Water dripping from pipes under the sink even when water is off
- Wet or warped cabinet bottom
- Musty smell from under the sink

Does NOT cover: drain clog (see KB-PLUMB-003), garbage disposal leak (see KB-PLUMB-009), faucet dripping above (see KB-PLUMB-006), water coming from the wall or ceiling into the cabinet (see KB-PLUMB-005).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. Is the water dripping when the faucet is on, when it's off, or both?
2. Can you see where it's coming from? The supply lines (small tubes going up to the faucet), the drain pipe (P-trap), or the faucet connections above? [PHOTO]
3. How much water? A few drops, steady drip, or pooling?
4. How long has it been happening? Is the cabinet bottom damaged?
5. Is there a garbage disposal? Is the leak near the disposal connections?

**Classification check**: Usually standard or urgent. Classify as urgent if:
- Water is actively pooling and the tenant cannot stop it
- Cabinet bottom is visibly damaged/warped (ongoing water damage)
- Leak is from the supply line (pressurized water, can get worse quickly)
- Leak is continuous even with the faucet off (supply line issue)

## Diagnostic decision tree

```
START
│
├─ Does the leak happen ONLY when the faucet is ON?
│   ├─ Likely a drain-side leak (P-trap, drain connections, disposal).
│   ├─ Can the tenant identify where it drips from? [PHOTO]
│   │   ├─ From the P-trap joints (the curved pipe below the drain)
│   │   │   └─ At Level 4+: try hand-tightening the slip nuts on the
│   │   │       P-trap (the large plastic or chrome rings). Turn clockwise.
│   │   │       Run water and check.
│   │   │       └─ If dripping stops → RESOLVED
│   │   │       └─ If still dripping → DISPATCH
│   │   ├─ From the drain tailpiece (straight pipe from sink to P-trap)
│   │   │   └─ At Level 4+: hand-tighten the connection where the
│   │   │       tailpiece meets the sink basket or disposal.
│   │   │       └─ If dripping stops → RESOLVED
│   │   │       └─ If still dripping → DISPATCH
│   │   ├─ From the disposal connections (if present)
│   │   │   └─ DISPATCH. Disposal seal replacement is not tenant-fixable.
│   │   └─ Cannot tell → DISPATCH
│   └─ At Level 3: ask tenant to dry everything under the sink with a
│       towel, place a dry paper towel under each connection, then run
│       water for 30 seconds. Check which paper towel gets wet.
│       → Helps identify the source for the vendor.
│
├─ Does the leak happen WHEN THE FAUCET IS OFF (or both)?
│   ├─ This is a SUPPLY LINE leak (pressurized water).
│   ├─ More urgent than a drain leak because the water keeps coming
│   │   regardless of use.
│   ├─ Can the tenant turn off the supply valves under the sink?
│   │   (Small knobs or handles on the tubes going to the wall.
│   │   Turn clockwise to close.)
│   │   └─ If valves close and dripping stops → supply line issue
│   │       confirmed. DISPATCH plumber.
│   │       (Tenant can turn valves back on briefly to use the sink,
│   │       then off again until repair.)
│   │   └─ If valves won't close (old, corroded, stuck) → DISPATCH
│   │       with urgency. Note: do NOT have tenant force stuck valves.
│   │   └─ If still dripping with valves closed → may be a valve
│   │       itself leaking. DISPATCH.
│   └─ If tenant cannot find or reach the valves → place towels/bucket
│       and DISPATCH with urgency.
│
├─ Is the CABINET BOTTOM damaged? (Warped, soft, swollen, moldy.)
│   └─ This indicates a long-term leak. The immediate fix is the plumbing,
│       but flag in work order that cabinet may need repair/replacement
│       afterward. Possible mold concern. Note for PM.
│
└─ If source cannot be identified → DISPATCH. Ask tenant to place
    towels/bucket and minimize use of that sink until the plumber arrives.
```

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

1. **Turn off supply valves** (if leak is from supply lines): Find the small valves under the sink where the lines go into the wall. Turn clockwise to close. This stops the leak immediately. Turn back on briefly when you need the sink.

2. **Hand-tighten P-trap connections** (Level 4+ only): The large slip nuts on the P-trap and drain connections can sometimes loosen over time. Turn them clockwise (hand-tight, do not use a wrench). Run water and check for drips.

3. **Identify the source**: Dry everything under the sink. Place dry paper towels under each connection. Run water for 30 seconds. Check which towel is wet. Report back.

## When to dispatch

- Supply line leak (pressurized, continuous)
- P-trap leak that doesn't resolve with hand-tightening
- Disposal connection leak
- Source unidentifiable
- Cabinet damage indicating long-term leak
- Tenant unable to shut off supply valves

## Vendor category to dispatch

Handyman for simple fixes (P-trap tightening, supply line replacement, slip-nut connections). Plumber for pipe corrosion, valve replacement, or anything inside the wall. Most under-sink leaks are handyman-level work. If cabinet repair is also needed, the same handyman can often handle that too.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- Shut off supply valves if the leak is supply-side (turn on briefly when needed)
- Place a bucket or towels under the active drip point
- Remove items stored under the sink to prevent water damage to tenant belongings
- Check the bucket/towels daily and replace as needed
- If cabinet bottom is wet, prop the cabinet doors open to allow air circulation and drying

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible. Pipe connections, supply lines, and drain fittings are building plumbing and always owner responsibility.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Which sink (kitchen, bathroom, which bathroom)
- Leak source if identified (supply line, P-trap, drain connection, disposal)
- Whether leak is continuous or only during use
- Whether supply valves are shut off
- Photo of the leak location
- Whether cabinet shows water damage
- Tenant availability

## Suggested tenant communication

**Supply line leak**: "Since it's dripping even with the faucet off, that's a pressurized line and I want to stop it now. Can you find the supply valves under the sink? They're small knobs where the lines go into the wall. Turn them clockwise. Once it stops dripping, you can turn them on briefly when you need the sink and off again when you're done. I'll get a plumber scheduled."

**Drain-side leak**: "It's good that it only happens when you're using the sink. It shouldn't get worse on its own. Put a small bucket or some towels under there for now and I'll get a plumber out within a couple of days."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Supply line leaks are more urgent than drain leaks. Supply lines are under pressure (constant water), while drain leaks only flow when the fixture is in use. Prioritize supply line leaks.
- Old supply line connections (especially braided stainless lines that are 10+ years old) can fail catastrophically. If the tenant describes a supply line that's "bulging" or "spraying," that's urgent -- a full burst can cause major flooding.
- If the cabinet shows mold or extensive water damage, flag for PM awareness. May require mold assessment depending on jurisdiction and scope.
- For multifamily: a first-floor unit reporting a leak under their kitchen sink may be unrelated to the unit above, or it could be their own supply/drain. Don't confuse this with a ceiling leak (KB-PLUMB-005).
- Standard repairs (supply line replacement, P-trap replacement, faucet connection repair) are usually within NTE.
