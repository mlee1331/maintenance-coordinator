# Pest Complaint (Roaches, Ants, Mice, Rats, Bedbugs)

## Symptoms covered

- Tenant reports seeing roaches (live or dead)
- Tenant reports ants in the unit
- Tenant reports mice or rats (droppings, sounds, sightings)
- Tenant reports bedbugs (bites, sightings, evidence on mattress)
- Tenant reports other pests (spiders, wasps, fleas, silverfish)

Does NOT cover: wildlife removal (raccoons, squirrels, birds in attic/walls, bats). Those require a wildlife removal specialist, not a pest control company. Dispatch directly for wildlife.

## Intake questions for tenant

1. What are you seeing? What type of pest? How many?
2. Where in the unit? One room or multiple areas? [PHOTO of evidence]
3. When did you first notice? Has it been getting worse?
4. Is this a single sighting or ongoing evidence (droppings, multiple sightings, damage)?
5. Have you tried any treatment yourself? (Sprays, traps, baits.)
6. For bedbugs specifically: are you getting bites? Have you traveled recently or received used furniture?
7. For multifamily: have any neighbors mentioned similar issues?

**Classification check**: Most pest issues are standard or urgent. Classify as urgent when:
- Active infestation with visible colony (roaches in numbers, mouse droppings throughout)
- Bedbugs confirmed (they spread rapidly, delay makes it worse)
- Rats (health hazard, potential structural damage)
- Wasps/bees near unit entrance or where children play
- Tenant has a health condition affected by pests (allergies, asthma triggered by roach allergens)

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

Most pest issues are not tenant-fixable once an infestation is established. Tenant actions are limited to prevention and monitoring:

1. **For isolated ant scouts** (Level 3+): Clean all food debris from counters and floors. Wipe ant trails with vinegar or soapy water. Store food in sealed containers. Seal visible entry points with tape temporarily. Monitor for 48 hours.

2. **For a single roach from outside**: Check under sinks and behind appliances for evidence of more. If none found, it was likely incidental. Monitor.

3. **For mice** (Level 4+): Remove accessible food sources. Place snap traps baited with peanut butter along walls where evidence was seen. Check daily.

## When to dispatch

- Any confirmed infestation (multiple sightings, droppings throughout, colony evidence)
- Bedbugs (always, immediately)
- Rats (always, urgently)
- Wasps/bees near high-traffic areas
- Roaches in numbers (more than one or two isolated sightings)
- Ants that persist after tenant cleaning efforts
- Tenant unable or unwilling to monitor/trap
- Any pest complaint in multifamily (risk of building-wide issue)

## Vendor category to dispatch

Pest control company. Not a handyman. Licensed pest control required for chemical or heat treatment. For wildlife (raccoons, squirrels, bats), use a wildlife removal specialist, not standard pest control.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- **Roaches/ants**: Clean thoroughly, seal food, reduce moisture sources (fix drips, run exhaust fans)
- **Mice**: Seal food in hard containers, place snap traps, stuff steel wool into any visible entry holes (mice can't chew through steel wool)
- **Bedbugs**: Do NOT move furniture between rooms. Do NOT discard mattress. Do NOT spray retail pesticides. Tenant can encase mattress in a bedbug-proof cover if they have one.
- **Wasps**: Avoid the area. Do not spray the nest with retail products (can agitate them and make professional removal harder).

## Tenant responsibility notes

Pest responsibility is one of the most jurisdiction-dependent areas in property management. General guidelines:

- **Roaches**: varies widely. Some jurisdictions hold the landlord responsible for multi-unit infestations regardless. Single-family may be tenant-responsible if the infestation is clearly caused by tenant cleanliness.
- **Ants**: usually owner-responsible for treatment, though prevention (food storage) is tenant's job.
- **Mice/rats**: almost always owner-responsible to remediate. Structural entry points are the owner's problem.
- **Bedbugs**: varies dramatically by state. Some states (NY, IL, several others) place full responsibility on the landlord. Others allow chargeback if the tenant can be shown to have introduced them (very hard to prove). Check jurisdiction.
- **Fleas**: often tenant-responsible if the tenant has pets. Owner-responsible if no pets and fleas came from a prior tenant.

**When in doubt**: dispatch, treat, and flag for PM to decide on responsibility/chargeback after the fact. Do not argue with the tenant about who caused it. Health and habitability come first.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Type of pest
- Evidence (sightings, droppings, bites, damage) with photos
- Which areas of the unit are affected
- How long the issue has been present
- Whether tenant has pets (relevant for fleas, and for treatment prep)
- Whether this is multifamily (building-wide treatment may be needed)
- Tenant availability (pest treatment often requires the tenant to be out for several hours)
- Any known allergies (bee stings, chemical sensitivities for treatment)

## Suggested tenant communication

**Acknowledging the issue**: "I know pests are stressful. Let me get pest control scheduled. In the meantime, make sure all food is sealed up and take out trash regularly. That won't fix it, but it slows them down."

**Bedbugs specifically**: "I want to get this handled right. I'm scheduling professional treatment. Important: please don't move your furniture to other rooms or throw anything away yet. The pest control company will give you specific prep instructions before they come. Moving things around can spread them."

**Tenant responsibility conversation**: "For this type of issue, I'm going to get it treated first because that's the priority. The property manager may follow up about responsibility, but right now let's focus on getting it resolved."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Pest responsibility is politically charged. Tenants feel strongly that they're not the cause. Do not accuse or imply blame. Treat first, sort responsibility later.
- Bedbugs are the most expensive and disruptive pest issue. Heat treatment is significantly more expensive than chemical treatment, but chemical treatment often requires multiple visits. Always flag for NTE and PM awareness.
- In multifamily, one unit's pest complaint often means the building has a problem. Alert the PM to consider building-wide inspection/treatment.
- Pest control often requires tenant prep (removing items from cabinets, bagging clothes, being out of the unit for hours). The coordinator should ensure the pest control company communicates prep instructions to the tenant with enough lead time.
- For recurring pest issues: if the same unit reports pests within 30 days of treatment, the pest control company should return under warranty. Check the vendor's warranty policy before dispatching again.
- Some tenants refuse chemical treatment due to health concerns (pregnant, children, pets, chemical sensitivity). Discuss with pest control company about alternative methods (traps, baits, gel treatments vs. spray).
