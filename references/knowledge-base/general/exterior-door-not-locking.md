# Exterior Door Not Locking or Closing Properly

## Symptoms covered

- Front door deadbolt won't engage
- Door won't latch closed (swings open on its own)
- Door frame damaged (strike plate pulled out, frame cracked)
- Door kicked in or forced open (break-in or attempted)
- Door lock cylinder damaged or won't accept key
- Door gap too large (visible daylight, security concern)

Does NOT cover: interior doors not latching (Low priority, handyman), screen door or storm door issues (Cosmetic), lock issues where door is secure but inconvenient (see KB-GEN-002), garage door issues (separate entry).

## Intake questions for tenant

1. Can you secure the door at all right now? (Deadbolt, chain lock, anything?)
2. Is the door frame damaged or just the lock?
3. Was there a break-in or attempted break-in? (If yes, have police been called?)
4. Is it nighttime or daytime?
5. Are you home alone? Do you feel safe?

**Classification check**: An exterior door that cannot be secured is a High severity security issue. Classify as Medium minimum. If it's nighttime, the tenant is alone, or there was a break-in attempt, classify per company's lockout/security policy and potentially as High. Dispatch same-day.

If there was a break-in or attempted break-in, advise tenant to call police FIRST if they haven't already. Then dispatch for emergency board-up/repair.

## Tenant-fixable resolution steps

Exterior door lock and frame issues are not tenant-fixable. The only tenant action is temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch.

## When to dispatch

- Always. Exterior door security issues are always dispatched.
- Same-day for any door that cannot be locked or latched.
- Emergency dispatch if break-in damage, nighttime, or tenant feels unsafe.

## Vendor category to dispatch

Locksmith for lock/deadbolt issues. Handyman for door alignment and minor frame repair. Major frame reconstruction (e.g., break-in damage) escalates to PM/owner for a specialty contractor (most operations keep no carpenter on staff). Secure the unit first; for emergency board-up, some locksmiths or handyman services offer this.

## Temporary mitigation while awaiting dispatch

- If a secondary lock still works (chain, deadbolt if knob is broken, or vice versa), use it.
- Prop a chair under the door handle to prevent it from being pushed open from outside.
- For doors that won't latch at all: if available, use a bungee cord or rope to tie the handle to something inside to keep it pulled closed.
- If the door cannot be closed at all (frame too damaged), the tenant should not stay in the unit. Offer to contact PM about temporary accommodation if dispatch is not possible tonight.

## Tenant responsibility notes

Never tenant-responsible unless the tenant caused the damage (kicked their own door in after lockout, for example). Even then, the repair is owner-coordinated. Exterior door security is a fundamental habitability requirement.

## Work order fields to capture at dispatch

- Nature of damage (lock, frame, door itself, strike plate)
- Whether the door can be closed at all
- Whether any lock still functions
- Whether there was a break-in (police report filed?)
- Photo of the damage
- Time of day and whether tenant is currently in the unit
- Whether tenant feels safe
- Tenant availability (for security issues, vendor may need to come even if tenant isn't there)

## Suggested tenant communication

**Security emergency**: "An exterior door that can't be secured is a top priority. I'm dispatching someone right now. In the meantime, prop a chair under the handle from inside and use your chain lock if you have one. Are you somewhere you feel safe?"

**After break-in**: "I'm sorry this happened. Have you been able to file a police report? That's important for both your records and the property's insurance. I'm sending someone to secure the door as quickly as possible. Don't touch or clean up the area around the door until police have seen it."

**Misalignment (less urgent)**: "I can tell the deadbolt isn't quite lining up. I'll get someone out today to adjust it. Until then, does the knob lock still work? If so, use that for now."

## Notes for coordinator agent

- Never leave a tenant with an unsecurable exterior door overnight. If same-day dispatch is not possible, escalate to PM for emergency board-up or temporary accommodation.
- Break-in damage may trigger an insurance claim. Notify PM immediately. The tenant should file a police report.
- Door frame damage from forced entry is common in affordable housing and older properties. If the frame has been repaired before (visible wood filler, reinforcement plate), it may need a more substantial fix this time. Note for the vendor.
- Emergency/after-hours rates are typically 2-3x daytime rates. Flag for NTE if extensive.
- In multifamily: if building entry doors are the issue (not individual unit doors), this affects all tenants and should be escalated to PM for building-wide security response.
